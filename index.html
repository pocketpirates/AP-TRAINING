The Indaver Rivenhall IWMF Safety Rules are provided to ensure work can be undertaken without hazard from electrical, mechanical, or associated plant equipment.

S Safety Rules are mandatory Click to reveal
Back

The Safety Rules are mandatory for work at Rivenhall IWMF. It is the duty of every person involved to be familiar with and comply with the Safety Rules, supporting documents, and relevant statutory requirements.

S Safety Rules do not make a job safe Click to reveal
Back

Safety Rules do not, by themselves, make a job safe. The achievement of safety from all inherent dangers also depends on full and logical preparation of work. This includes identifying and assessing all reasonably foreseeable risks, implementing suitable control measures, and ensuring the work is carried out safely by competent personnel.

No work is so urgent or important that there is no time to plan it properly, assess the risks, and carry it out safely.

A Assessing risk Click to reveal
Back

A risk assessment is a careful examination of all reasonably foreseeable risks associated with the task and the inherent risks present in the system in its operational state, to reduce the risk of injury or ill health to a level as low as reasonably practicable.

D Disciplinary for non-compliance Click to reveal
Back

Failure to comply with the Safety Rules, associated procedures, or safety documentation may result in disciplinary action in accordance with company policy.

P Policy Click to reveal
Back

It is Indaver Group Policy that a health and safety management system is in place, with legal compliance regarded as the minimum standard. The Safety Rules form part of this system and are supported by company procedures and guidance documents.

P Philosophy Click to reveal
Back

Plant and/or apparatus may form interconnected systems containing inherent dangers. In normal operation they can be operated safely if procedures are followed. When work departs from normal operation, Safety Rules and Safe Systems of Work are required to protect people from those inherent dangers, and additional SSoWs may be needed for hazards outside the system.

P Principles Click to reveal
Back

The Safety Rules are based on establishing a Safe System of Work before work starts; achieving safety primarily through controlled isolation and control of stored energy; restricting live work unless justified with precautions; and ensuring work is carried out by competent, authorised persons using the correct documentation and procedures.

Def What does “shall” mean? Click to reveal
Back

When “shall” is used, the requirement is mandatory. It must be complied with and no discretion or judgement is permitted.

Def What does “shall where practicable” mean? Click to reveal
Back

“Shall where practicable” means the requirement must be met wherever it is possible to do so, taking into account current knowledge and the hazards involved. The requirement cannot be avoided due to difficulty, inconvenience, or cost.

Def What does “shall where reasonably practicable” mean? Click to reveal
Back

“Shall where reasonably practicable” means a balanced judgement must be made, weighing the level of risk against the time, cost, effort, and trouble required to reduce it.

Def What does the term “work” include? Click to reveal
Back

In these Safety Rules, the term “work” may also include testing.

Roles

Definitions of key roles and responsibilities under the Safety Rules.

Role What is a Working Party? Click to reveal
Back

A Working Party is the persons working under the supervision of an Acceptor, including an Acceptor working alone.

Doc What is a Multiple Working Party Safety Document? Click to reveal
Back

A Multiple Working Party Safety Document allows one or more working parties to carry out work at the same time under the same Safety Document. Only a Limited Work Certificate or a Permit For Work are permitted in this format. A Permit ROS or Sanction For Operation and Testing is not permitted and must be a Single Working Party Document.

Doc What is a Single Working Party Safety Document? Click to reveal
Back

A Single Working Party Safety Document allows only one working party to carry out work under it at any one time.

Sup What is Supervision? Click to reveal
Back

Supervision under the Safety Rules means either Immediate Supervision or Personal Supervision, depending on the level required.

Sup What is Immediate Supervision? Click to reveal
Back

Immediate Supervision is supervision by a person who is continually available at the location when work or testing is in progress and who attends the work area as often as necessary to ensure the work is carried out safely.

Sup What is Personal Supervision? Click to reveal
Back

Personal Supervision is supervision where the supervising person is present at the work location, observing the work, and able to intervene.

Role What is a Plant Controller? Click to reveal
Back

A Plant Controller is a person authorised in writing by the Location Plant Manager, responsible for the release of plant and/or apparatus at the location for work and for coordinating safety activities at system interfaces with other companies. The Plant Controller may also fulfil the role of Safety Controller, Appointed Person, or Acceptor.

Role What is a Safety Controller? Click to reveal
Back

A Safety Controller is a person authorised in writing by the Location Plant Manager who is responsible for ensuring adequate precautions are taken to provide safety from the system. This includes, where appropriate, preparing and cancelling a specified Safety Document. The Safety Controller may also fulfil the role of Appointed Person or Acceptor.

Role What is an Issuer? Click to reveal
Back

An Issuer is a person authorised in writing by the Location Plant Manager to issue and transfer a specified Safety Document (as part of the Safety Controller arrangements).

Role What is an Appointed Person? Click to reveal
Back

An Appointed Person is a person nominated by the Location Plant Manager to carry out specified duties relating to safety from the system. An Appointed Person may also fulfil the role of Acceptor.

Role What is an Acceptor? Click to reveal
Back

An Acceptor is a person nominated by the Location Plant Manager to provide supervision to members of a Working Party and to accept, surrender, transfer, and clear specified Safety Documents.

Confined Space

Key confined space hazards, categories, responsibilities, and controls.

Hazards What hazards are likely in a Confined Space? Click to reveal
Back

Hazards in a Confined Space may include oxygen deficiency, oxygen enrichment, hazardous gases or vapours from processes, residues or sludge, fumes generated by work activities, fire or explosion, heat stress, drowning from liquids, engulfment by solids, asphyxiation, entrapment, and restricted access or egress.

Levels How many Confined Space categories are there? Click to reveal
Back

There are four categories of Confined Space: Levels 1 to 4, ranging from low specified risk to high risk.

CS 1 What is a Level 1 Confined Space? Click to reveal
Back

A Level 1 Confined Space is low specified risk with a shallow entry of less than 1200mm, adequate natural or mechanical ventilation, simple and unobstructed access, and no likely risk of flooding.

CS 2 What is a Level 2 Confined Space? Click to reveal
Back

A Level 2 Confined Space is low specified risk with reasonable access and egress. Persons may remain attached to a safety line at all times, and escape breathing apparatus may be required.

CS 3 What is a Level 3 Confined Space? Click to reveal
Back

A Level 3 Confined Space is medium risk with difficult access or egress where permanent attachment to a safety line is not possible. Operatives must have escape sets. The requirement for a rescue team is determined by risk assessment.

CS 4 What is a Level 4 Confined Space? Click to reveal
Back

A Level 4 Confined Space is high risk, with difficult access or egress, multiple obstacles, heat stress, or known hazards. All operatives must be trained in the use of breathing apparatus, and a full external rescue team with BA capability must be on standby.

Responsibility Who is responsible once a Confined Space Permit is issued? Click to reveal
Back

The Acceptor is responsible once the Confined Space Permit has been issued.

Responsibility Who arranges a Standby Man? Click to reveal
Back

The Acceptor is responsible for arranging a Standby Man.

Testing Who is responsible for atmospheric testing? Click to reveal
Back

The Appointed Person is responsible for completing atmospheric testing when required.

Notice Who posts Confined Space notices? Click to reveal
Back

The Safety Controller is responsible for posting Confined Space notices at access points.

Control How must access doors and covers be controlled? Click to reveal
Back

Access doors and covers to Confined Spaces must be locked in the open position or removed to prevent them from being closed.

Prohibition Can gas cylinders be taken into a Confined Space? Click to reveal
Back

No. Gas cylinders must not be taken into a Confined Space.

Standby What are the key responsibilities of a Standby Man? Click to reveal
Back

The Standby Man must remain outside the Confined Space at all times, never enter the space, maintain communication, keep records of persons inside, monitor the Gas Free Certificate, ensure notices are in place, respond to emergencies, keep access points clear, and remain present and attentive at all times.

Closure What are the Acceptor’s responsibilities when closing a Confined Space? Click to reveal
Back

The Acceptor must ensure all tools and equipment are removed, withdraw the working party, prevent re-entry, and clear the Safety Document.

Closure What are the Safety Controller’s responsibilities when closing a Confined Space? Click to reveal
Back

Once all Safety Documents are cleared, the Safety Controller must arrange a final inspection under appropriate safety arrangements, remove the Confined Space notice, and ensure the space is closed. If closure is delayed, suitable barriers and signage must be put in place.

Hot Works

Hazards, responsibilities, gas testing, monitoring, and what to do if hot work is identified mid-task.

Definition What is the definition of a Hot Work? Click to reveal

Any temporary operation involving the introduction of open flames or production of heat or sparks, including, but not limited to: Brazing, Cutting, Grinding, Soldering or Welding.

Hazards Hot Work has inherent hazards with various effects, please describe? Click to reveal

Fire caused by heat transfer onto a flammable material.

Explosion caused by flammable materials reaching their explosive limit.

Burns caused by heat transfer from work area.

Poisonous fumes or vapours produced by work carried out (such as welding, grinding, or cutting certain materials with galvanised coatings).

Oxygen depletion caused by welding/cutting work.

Arc eye, corneal burns caused by ultraviolet radiation during welding.

Process If a Hot Work is identified during the Work process what are the requirements? Click to reveal

New RAMS are required to include the Hot Work.

The original Safety Document is cancelled.

The Safety Controller prepares a new Safety Document for the task including Hot Work.

The Acceptor receives the new Safety Document.

A Hot Work Assessment is carried out, issued and posted at the work site.

Role Who is in charge of Hot Works once a Permit is issued? Click to reveal

The person in charge of the hot work activities shall be an Acceptor – Hot Work and shall be in receipt of a Safety Document and a Hot Work Certificate before allowing Hot Work to commence.

Assessment What are two considerations a Appointed Person should consider when asked to carry out a Hot Work assessment? Click to reveal

Responsible for reviewing the task and considering if there is a practicable alternative to hot work.

Isolations

Definitions, roles, key safes/locks/tags, and mechanical/electrical isolation hazards and controls.

Principle What is the primary means of achieving Safety from the System? Click to reveal
Back

Safety from the System is primarily achieved by controlled isolation from the System and control of stored energy.

Rules What does “shall” mean? Click to reveal
Back

“Shall” means it must be done — no discretion or judgement is allowed.

Rules What does “shall where practicable” (SWP) mean? Click to reveal
Back

SWP means if it can be done, it must be done — it cannot be avoided due to time, cost, or effort.

Rules What does “shall where reasonably practicable” (SWRP) mean? Click to reveal
Back

SWRP means judgement is allowed — balancing the level of risk against the time, cost, effort, and trouble needed to reduce it.

Defs What is the difference between Plant and Apparatus? Click to reveal
Back

Apparatus is anything containing an electrical conductor. Plant is everything else.

Defs What is the definition of “Isolated”? Click to reveal
Back

Isolated means disconnected from associated Plant and/or Apparatus by an isolating device in the isolating position, or by adequate physical separation or sufficient gap.

Defs What is the definition of “Drained”? Click to reveal
Back

Drained means the contents of Plant and/or Apparatus are adjusted and maintained at a level which avoids Danger.

Defs What is the definition of “Vented”? Click to reveal
Back

Vented means the contents of Plant and/or Apparatus are adjusted and maintained at atmospheric pressure.

Defs What is the definition of “Purged”? Click to reveal
Back

Purged means Plant and/or Apparatus is in a condition where any dangerous contents have been removed.

Tag What is a Safety Tag? Click to reveal
Back

A Safety Tag is an approved means of identifying locally the point of application of safety precautions.

Voltage What is Low Voltage (LV)? Click to reveal
Back

Low voltage is anything below 1000V.

Form What is included on a Declaration of Isolation form? Click to reveal
Back

Declaration of Isolation includes: Key Safe number, key number(s), Safety Document number, points of isolation and descriptions, isolated position, Appointed Person signature, reinstatement signature, and sign-off signatures at the bottom of the document.

Keys Name the different types of keys used. Click to reveal
Back

Key Safe Key: unique at the location, operates a Key Safe lock other than the control lock. Control Key: issued solely to Safety Controllers to operate the control lock of a Key Safe. Safety Key: unique at the location, operates a safety lock that locks an isolating device/earthing device/vent/drain.

Device What is a Key Safe? Click to reveal
Back

A Key Safe is an approved device for the secure retention of keys.

Locks Describe Control Lock, Personal Lock, and Safety Lock. Click to reveal
Back

Control Lock: a lock on a Key Safe or Card Safe that only Safety Controllers have a key for. Personal Lock: unique at the location, issued to an Acceptor for securing Safety Documents in a Card Safe. Safety Lock: an approved device for securing points of isolation, operated by a unique Safety Key.

Danger Name some inherent dangers in the system. Click to reveal
Back

Examples include electricity, moving parts, springs, capacitors, stored pressure, and gravity.

Role What are the Safety Controller’s responsibilities for isolations? Click to reveal
Back

The Safety Controller ensures the procedure is followed and appoints the Appointed Person – Isolations to carry out isolation and reinstatement of plant and/or apparatus as required.

Role What are the Appointed Person – Isolations responsibilities? Click to reveal
Back

The Appointed Person – Isolations receives instruction from the Safety Controller to carry out isolations as per the Declaration of Isolation, including sequence and method, and reinstates plant and/or apparatus when authorised.

Role What are the Acceptor’s responsibilities during isolations? Click to reveal
Back

The Acceptor liaises with the Appointed Person – Isolations before isolation takes place. The Acceptor does not carry out isolations and must not alter them. If changes are required or work is complete, the Appointed Person – Isolations must be called to make the change or remove isolation.

Mechanical What hazards must be released for mechanical machinery? Click to reveal
Back

Residual energy must be released: Mechanical—allow rotating parts to run down and release springs; Electrical—discharge capacitors, disconnect/remove batteries; Hydraulic—depressurise accumulators and pipework; Pneumatic—depressurise systems and disconnect lines if trapped air could operate valves; Services—steam/gas/fuel may need depressurising, venting, purging, or draining; Also consider gravity—engineered chocks may be required.

Electrical What are the hazards and key controls for electrical equipment? Click to reveal
Back

Electrical hazards include electric shock, burns, and arcing that can ignite flammable materials. Isolate the main power circuit and any hazardous auxiliary circuits, disconnect and separate from every source of energy, and discharge stored energy (especially batteries and capacitors).

Devices Name devices suitable for electrical isolation. Click to reveal
Back

Circuit breakers with required contact separation and locking; disconnectors/isolators with locking; switch disconnectors with locking; plug and socket outlets; fuse links; removable links.

Rule What do the rules say about isolating mechanical plant? Click to reveal
Back

Mechanical plant and equipment isolating devices shall, where practicable, be secured in the isolated position using a Safety Lock (or locks) and Appendix A Safety Tag(s) applied. Isolations are determined by the Safety Controller using methods such as P&IDs, site inspections, and documentation.

Physical What is classed as a physical isolation? Click to reveal
Back

Examples of physical isolation include valves, spades, and blank flanges.

LV How should LV isolating devices be secured (SWP)? Click to reveal
Back

LV isolating devices shall, where practicable, be secured in the isolated position by a Safety Lock (or locks) and an Appendix A Safety Tag applied.

Exception If a Safety Lock is not reasonably practicable, what do you do? Click to reveal
Back

Inform the Safety Controller.

Alt Control If a Safety Lock isn’t reasonably practicable, how can work proceed safely? Click to reveal
Back

Once the Safety Controller has been informed, the LV isolation device may be deemed locked once safety tape has been applied and it is tagged with an Appendix B Safety Tag.

Tags What is the difference between Appendix A and Appendix B Safety Tags? Click to reveal
Back

Appendix B Safety Tags specify the Safety Document type and document number.

Scenario A Safety Controller instructs you to carry out an isolation — describe the process. Click to reveal
Back

Use a structured answer: confirm scope and plant/apparatus; identify all energy sources (including stored energy and interlocks); follow the Declaration of Isolation sequence; apply isolations, locks/keys, and safety tags; release/secure stored energy (drain, vent, purge as required); prove isolation where applicable; record details on the Declaration of Isolation; maintain control until authorised reinstatement; and reinstate only when instructed and safe to do so.
