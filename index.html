Def What does “shall” mean? Click to reveal
Back

When “shall” is used, the requirement is mandatory. It must be complied with and no discretion or judgement is permitted.

Def What does “shall where practicable” mean? Click to reveal
Back

“Shall where practicable” means the requirement must be met wherever it is possible to do so, taking into account current knowledge and the hazards involved. The requirement cannot be avoided due to difficulty, inconvenience, or cost.

Def What does “shall where reasonably practicable” mean? Click to reveal
Back

“Shall where reasonably practicable” means a balanced judgement must be made, weighing the level of risk against the time, cost, effort, and trouble required to reduce it.

Def What does the term “work” include? Click to reveal

In these Safety Rules, the term “work” may also include testing.

Confined Space

Key confined space hazards, categories, responsibilities, and controls.

Hazards What are the hazards likely to be present in a Confined Space? Click to reveal
  • Oxygen deficiency, for example by insufficient Purging of contents and replacement with normal air.
  • Gas or vapour remaining from the process normally ongoing in that space.
  • Fumes or vapours emitted from sludge, deposits or residues remaining in the space.
  • Fumes or vapours produced by work carried out within the space such as welding, burning, painting or cleaning activities.
  • Oxygen enrichment following work, which requires an excess of oxygen such as oxyacetylene cutting.
  • Serious injury from fire or explosion.
  • Loss of consciousness arising from heat stress.
  • Drowning as a result of engulfment by liquids.
  • Asphyxiation or entrapment as a result of engulfment by solids.
  • Entry in to spaces where there is limited or restricted access and egress.
Levels How many levels of CS categories do we have? And explain each level? Click to reveal

4

1 – Low specified risk

  • shallow entry of less than 1200mm
  • adequate natural or mechanical ventilation
  • Access should be simple and unobstructed
  • there is no likely risk of flooding

2- Low specified risk

  • reasonable access / egress
  • the persons may remain attached to the safety line at all times
  • Escape breathing apparatus maybe required for operatives

3- Medium Risk confined space

  • difficult access / egress
  • it is not possible for persons to be permanently attached to a safety line
  • Operatives must have escape sets
  • The requirement for a rescue team is determined by Risk assessment

4 - High Risk confined space

  • difficult access/egress
  • multiple obstacles
  • Heat Stress
  • known risk may be from many aspects or workload could cause exhaustion
  • All operatives must be trained in the use of breathing apparatus
  • Full external rescue team with BA capability must be on standby

Standby What are the responsibilities of a stand by man? Click to reveal

The Standby Man shall;

Remain at the entry point outside and never under any circumstances enter the Confined Space.

Have an adequate means of communicating with person(s) within the Confined Space and with the Control Room in case of emergency.

Be competent to use the site radio and be aware of emergency contact numbers.

Keep a physical record of all persons inside the Confined Space at any one time.

Be aware of the Gas Free Certificate & if it is due to expire he shall ensure all persons exit the Confined Space and inform the Acceptor in charge of the work.

Be aware of the Confined Space Notice(s) & check that the notice(s) are in place before allowing persons to enter.

If the Site Emergency Alarms sound, ensure all persons within the Confined Space leave immediately and report with them to the appropriate muster point.

Be present at all times whenever there is entry into a confined space.

Ensure Access & Egress points are kept clear at all times.

Not allow himself to be distracted from monitoring personnel in the Confined Space.

Withdraws his Working Party and instructs them not to re-enter, then clears his Safety Document.

Closure Safety Controller responsibilities? Click to reveal

Hot Works

Hazards, responsibilities, gas testing, monitoring, and what to do if hot work is identified mid-task.

Definition What is the definition of a Hot Work? Click to reveal

Any temporary operation involving the introduction of open flames or production of heat or sparks, including, but not limited to: Brazing, Cutting, Grinding, Soldering or Welding.

Hazards Hot Work has inherent hazards with various effects, please describe? Click to reveal

Fire caused by heat transfer onto a flammable material.

Explosion caused by flammable materials reaching their explosive limit.

Burns caused by heat transfer from work area.

Poisonous fumes or vapours produced by work carried out (such as welding, grinding, or cutting certain materials with galvanised coatings).

Oxygen depletion caused by welding/cutting work.

Arc eye, corneal burns caused by ultraviolet radiation during welding.

The original Safety Document is cancelled.

The Safety Controller prepares a new Safety Document for the task including Hot Work.

A Hot Work Assessment is carried out, issued and posted at the work site.

Where the contents of the Plant and/or Apparatus are adjusted and maintained at atmospheric pressure.

A condition of Plant and/or Apparatus from which any dangerous contents have been removed.

Danger Name the inherent Dangers in the system? Click to reveal

Electricity, Moving parts, Springs, capacitors, slag etc

Mechanical Hazards associated with mechanical machinery? Click to reveal

Any residual mechanical, electrical or pressure energy which may be locked within any part of the machinery mechanism should be released as follows;

Mechanical – high and low speed rotating elements need to be run down and springs released;

Electrical – capacitors should be discharged and batteries disconnected and/or removed;

Hydraulic – accumulators and pressurised pipework should be depressurised;

Pneumatic – the system should be depressurised. If valves could be operated by residual trapped air, the line should also be disconnected;

Services – Steam, gas or fuel may need to be depressurised, vented, purged or drained.

In addition – Also be aware of the potential of gravity. An engineered chock may also be required.

Electrical Hazards associated with electrical equipment? Click to reveal

Hazards to workers include electric shock, electrical burns and electrical arcing resulting in the ignition of flammable gas, vapours or materials,

The main power circuit of the electrical equipment, plus any associated auxiliary circuits which constitute a hazard, should be electrically isolated. Disconnect and separate the electrical equipment from every source of electrical energy. Discharge any stored energy in the electrical circuits, taking particular care with batteries and capacitors.

Devices suitable for isolation include;

Circuit breakers with the required contact separation and locking facilities;

Disconnectors (commonly known as isolators) with locking facilities;

Switch disconnectors with locking facilities;

Plug and socket outlets;

Fuse links;

Removeable links.
